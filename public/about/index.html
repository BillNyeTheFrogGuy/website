About

Bio

Daniel Lazzaretto is a Southern California musician and audio engineer, currently completing a B.A. in Music from UC Irvine. Primarily a jazz saxophone performer, his main influence is the bop of the late 50s and early 60s, taking inspiration from artists such as Cannonball Adderley, Sonny Stitt, and Hank Mobley. A multi-instrumentalist, Lazzaretto is proficient on flute, piano, and percussion. He has studied with prominent Los Angeles artists Danny Janklow, Kirsten Edkins, and Jerry Pinter.

Starting with piano at age 7, Daniel picked up saxophone at age 10 and soon after joined a youth jazz combo. Throughout high school, he participated in the school's jazz band and performed for events at Pasadena City Hall and the Wrigley Mansion. In 2023, he earned the Louis Armstrong Award for outstanding musical achievement. He also arranged Jun Fukamachi's "On the Move" for big band, and performed it at graduation. Daniel participated in marching band on percussion, playing for 5 seasons and eventually becoming a front ensemble captain his senior year. He was also active in the high school musical theater pit orchestra, participating in 4 productions including PUSD's All-Star production of "Into the Woods". During his time in the pit orchestra he performed on keyboard and saxophone, as well as learning flute and clarinet.

At UC Irvine, Daniel studied privately with Jerry Pinter, and has taken ensemble and theory classes from Pinter, Sasha Berliner, Darek Oleszkiewicz, Paul Cornish, and Jason Harnell. He regularly performs in UCI's jazz combos and orchestra, as well as multiple ensembles outside of school such as the Bugs Quintet and the O.K. Big Band.
